# Research report #3: Nov 9, 2026

## The short version
1. **Carousels with 5-6 slides do best on TikTok, per one 2026 study.** TT Calculator (Feb 2026, 800+ accounts, not affiliated with TikTok) found 5-6 slide carousels averaged 7.1% engagement and 65% completion, vs 4.0% and 24% completion for 10+ slides. Carousels got more saves than video (2.9% vs 1.8%) but slightly fewer shares. Education posts gained the most. Our 5-slide how-to format is right; keep it. Treat the numbers as directional (unnamed sample).
2. **"Carousels beat single images by 44%"** is going around (Influencers Time, Oct 12, 2026), but it names no source and is mostly about Instagram and LinkedIn. Don't quote it.
3. **Falls are the big safety story.** OSHA's campaign page: falls are the leading cause of death in construction, 389 fatal falls out of 1,034 construction deaths in 2024 (BLS). Recent roofing cases: a Florida roofer facing $349,754 in proposed fines (July 2026), $282,420 proposed by Cal/OSHA after a fatal 24 ft roof fall in Los Angeles (Aug 2026), and a Mississippi roofer whose penalties went from $12,295 to $82,182 after a repeat citation.
4. **Our own numbers:** both product carousels beat our TikTok average. The one ending in a question got the comments. Watch time is short, so slide 1 needs a reason to swipe.

## Growth ideas
1. **"My prompt said 'make it epic'"** carousel for creators: vague prompt vs. a full prompt (subject, style, light, camera, mood). Ends with Prompt Studio and a one-word question. (Used this week.)
2. **"Up on a roof today?"** fall rules in plain English for crews, ending with the JHA form and "$9.99 once, no monthly fee." (Used this week, plus the weekly blog.)
3. **Save-bait checklist post** next: a single "pre-shift JHA checklist" carousel people save for later, since saves are where carousels win.
