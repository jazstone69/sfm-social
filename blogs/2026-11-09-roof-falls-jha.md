# Up on a roof? What three 2026 roofing cases teach about planning for falls

*Stone Familia Media safety blog, Nov 9, 2026*

Falls are the leading cause of death in construction. OSHA says that in 2024 there were 389 fatal falls to a lower level out of 1,034 construction deaths, and calls them preventable ([OSHA Stop Falls](https://www.osha.gov/stop-falls)). This year's roofing cases show the same pattern: the hazard was known, and the plan wasn't there.

## Case 1: Florida, $349,754 proposed
In July 2026 OSHA cited Orchids Builders LLC of Florida for two willful and four repeat violations and proposed $349,754 in penalties. Inspectors found workers installing roof sheathing about 10 feet up with no fall protection in January, and workers installing hurricane clips about 9 feet up without it in March. They also found no written fall protection training records, no eye protection with nail guns, and ladders that didn't reach 36 inches above the roof landing. OSHA said the company had been inspected seven times since 2023, and every case included fall protection violations ([OSHA news release, July 23, 2026](https://www.osha.gov/news/newsreleases/atlanta/20260723)).

## Case 2: Los Angeles, $282,420 proposed
On Jan. 9, 2026, a worker repairing a roof leak at a two-story home in Westchester fell about 24 feet and died. Cal/OSHA proposed $282,420 against three companies for missing fall protection, gaps in ladder safety training and no certified first-aid worker on site, among other items. Two of the companies have appealed ([Construction Owners, Aug 2026](https://www.constructionowners.com/news/cal-osha-proposes-282-420-in-penalties-following-fatal-roofing-fall-in-los-angeles-county)).

## Case 3: Mississippi, from $12,295 to $82,182
Roofing Contractor reports that Chavez Roofing LLC was cited $12,295 in 2025 for roof fall protection at an Alabama jobsite. A few months later a worker died after a fall at a Mississippi job. Because it was a repeat violation, the proposed penalties jumped to $82,182; a settlement later reduced them to $41,091 ([Roofing Contractor, June 2026](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/102329-avoid-massive-osha-roofing-fines-how-12k-penalties-surged-to-82k)).

## The rules in plain English
- **6 feet.** In construction, workers on a surface with an unprotected edge 6 feet or more above a lower level need fall protection: guardrails, safety nets, or a personal fall arrest system (29 CFR 1926.501(b)(1)).
- **3 feet above the landing.** A portable ladder used to reach a roof should extend at least 3 feet above the landing (29 CFR 1926.1053(b)(1)).
- **Plan, Provide, Train.** OSHA's three steps: plan the job and the equipment before you start, provide the right gear (including a harness for every worker who ties off), and train everyone to use it and spot hazards ([OSHA Stop Falls](https://www.osha.gov/stop-falls)).

## Where a JHA helps
A Job Hazard Analysis is the "Plan" step on paper. Break the roof job into steps (set ladder, climb, carry materials, work the edge, work near skylights), write down the fall hazard for each, and pick the control before anyone climbs. Then the whole crew reviews it together. In all three cases above, the hazard was a plain one: an open roof edge. A five-minute JHA asks the question "how is nobody falling off this?" before the first shingle goes up.
