# Cold job today? What OSHA says about cold stress and winter hazards

*Stone Familia Media safety blog, Nov 16, 2026*

Winter doesn't stop the work. It just adds hazards that are easy to miss when the crew is in a hurry to get warm. Here's what OSHA says, in plain English, and how to put it in your Job Hazard Analysis.

## It doesn't have to be freezing
OSHA lists four kinds of cold stress: trench foot, frostbite, hypothermia and chilblains ([OSHA cold stress](https://www.osha.gov/winter-weather/cold-stress)). Two surprise people:

- **Trench foot** can happen at temperatures as high as 60°F if feet stay wet, because wet feet lose heat 25 times faster than dry feet. Signs include redness, numbness, tingling, swelling and blisters.
- **Hypothermia** (body temperature below 95°F) can happen above 40°F if a worker gets chilled by rain, sweat or cold water. Uncontrollable shivering is an early sign; confusion, slurred speech and loss of coordination mean it's serious.

Wind makes it worse. OSHA's example: at 40°F with a 35 mph wind, it feels like 28°F on exposed skin.

## What to do if someone's in trouble
- **Frostbite:** get them to a warm room, don't rub the area, and leave re-warming to medical professionals.
- **Hypothermia:** call 911, move them somewhere warm, replace wet clothes, and start CPR if there's no pulse.

(Both from [OSHA cold stress](https://www.osha.gov/winter-weather/cold-stress).)

## Other winter hazards on the job
OSHA's winter hazards page lists more than cold ([OSHA winter weather hazards](https://www.osha.gov/winter-weather/hazards)):

- **Snow on roofs.** A competent person should inspect a snow-loaded roof before anyone goes up. Snow can hide skylights. Methods that keep workers off the roof are preferred.
- **Slips on ice.** Clear walkways and spread deicer right after a storm. Wear insulated, water-resistant boots with good tread, and take short, slow steps.
- **Snow blowers.** Never clear a jam by hand. Turn it off and wait for parts to stop.
- **Downed power lines.** Treat every line as energized and stay clear.
- **Winter driving.** Keep vehicles maintained and carry a kit: shovel, traction aids, blankets, water and a phone.

## What employers are expected to do
OSHA has no specific cold-weather standard, but the general duty to keep workers safe still applies. OSHA recommends training workers to spot cold stress, using the buddy system, scheduling work in the warmest part of the day, giving frequent short breaks in warm, dry areas, and providing warm, sweet, non-alcoholic drinks ([OSHA cold stress](https://www.osha.gov/winter-weather/cold-stress)).

## Put it in your JHA
Add a "weather" line to every winter JHA:

| Job step | Hazard | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Work in wet trench or rain | Trench foot, hypothermia | Waterproof boots, spare dry socks, warm-up breaks |
| Access snowy roof | Hidden skylights, slips, falls | Competent person inspects first, fall arrest, non-slip boots |
| Walk the site after a storm | Slips on ice | Clear and deice paths, short steps |

Five minutes at the truck before the shift beats an ambulance call at noon.
